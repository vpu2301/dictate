{# _layout.html — the frame every marketing email shares. WHY A LAYOUT AND NOT NINE COPIES. The rule in this directory used to be "whole documents, so a translator who does not read Python can proofread them". That rule is about the COPY, and it survives: every translated sentence still lives in its own per-language file, inside a block. What moved out is the chrome — palette, typography, the card, the footer — which was identical in nine files and therefore drifted in nine files. Restyling the brand is now one edit instead of nine. THE DESIGN IS THE WEBSITE'S. The old letters were a separate identity: a cream ground (#E6E1D6), a terracotta accent and Georgia headlines, none of which appears anywhere on klarnote.com. These use the marketing site's own tokens (src/app-extra.css `.lp`): paper #FFFFFF, the one grey #F5F6F5, the hairline #E3E6E4, ink #1B2422 for headlines, #4A5450 for lead paragraphs, and the ink band #141B19 for the primary button — the same inversion the site's CTA band uses. FONTS, and why the display type is a picture. Geist for text and Galgo for the headline, self-hosted at the public origin and declared here as @font-face. That reaches Apple Mail, iOS Mail and Thunderbird — and nothing else: Gmail and Outlook strip @font-face outright, so in the clients these letters are mostly read in, Galgo had never rendered once. The two places the brand actually lives, the wordmark and the hero headline, are therefore pre-rendered PNGs attached as `cid:` parts (adapters/mail_images.py, and tools/render_mail_images.py for the rest of the reasoning). Everything else stays live text: a letter set entirely in images is unreadable with images off, unselectable, untranslatable and a spam signal. The text stacks below still matter — they set every other line, and they are what the headline falls back to when a language has no PNG yet. Galgo is a condensed display face, so its chain reaches for other condensed faces first (Arial Narrow, Haettenschweiler) before giving up to Geist: a condensed headline that falls back to a normal one changes the line count. Outlook is given Arial outright through an mso conditional, because its font stack parsing is not to be trusted with four fallbacks. `public_base_url` arrives through the render fields. It is filtered through `default` on purpose: the outbox freezes render_fields at enqueue time, so a mail queued by the previous deploy has no such key, and StrictUndefined would fail its retry rather than send it. #} {% set base = public_base_url | default('https://klarnote.com', true) -%} {# `| safe` HERE, on the definition, not on the 159 use sites in the twelve letter files.
